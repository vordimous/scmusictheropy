## Course Description

The end of life offers us much richness by way of reflections, perspective shifts, complex emotions, and vulnerability. Music meets this time of a person's life with equal amounts of richness, both affirming and expanding the experience of the dying journey. While many different types of music experiences are utilized in the end of life, this CMTE will explore the intervention of songwriting specifically. Together, participants will learn about the aspects of songwriting and how they are related to aspects of humanity and therapeutic relationship, and in doing so they will be challenged to shift from a focus on preconceived plans to a focus on in-the-moment reflecting and creating.

There will be two informational lectures separated by a ten-minute break followed by an experiential component, where participants are able to implement and practice what they learn.

**Equipment:** Having access to a musical instrument of choice will be required for this CMTE in order to participate in the experiential portion appropriately. (If you are unable to bring your own instrument, please indicate on your registration and we will let you know what is available in the classroom, on loan from Prisma Midlands Music Therapy.)

## Learner Objectives

Upon completion of this CMTE, participants will be able to:

1. **Explore** at least 3 musical elements and practice implementing them in songwriting experiences (3A-11).

2. **Identify** 2 or more ways that music-centered, humanistic approaches inform songwriting and overall therapeutic relationship (3A-6).

3. **Learn and articulate** ways to acknowledge their own personal bias, tendencies, and musical leanings in session (5A-2).

**Prerequisites:** There are no prerequisites to attend this CMTE event. Students are welcome and encouraged to come and learn with MT-BCs.

## About the Presenter

**Sarah Mumford, MT-BC** is a Board-Certified music therapist, working predominantly in hospice and palliative care. Over the past 9 years, she has also practiced within the realms of grief support, veteran care, and cancer survivorship, and she has fostered her own relationship with music as a singer-songwriter. Sarah practices within a humanistic, music-centered approach and believes deeply that music is one of the most powerful mediums for therapeutic change within those we serve, within ourselves, and within the world around us. Sarah is based in the Greenville, SC area, where she currently works as a bereavement counselor for Carolina Caring.
